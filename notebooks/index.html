WNBA Contract Value • Retrospective Accuracy Check

Was the Undervalued Contract Model Right?

This editorial notebook revisits a class-project claim: four WNBA players looked underpaid relative to role, production, and comparable players. The follow-up asks whether the next salary window and another season of performance data confirmed, complicated, or corrected the original read.

Read the bars correctly

All percentile bars shown in the player cards are percentiles by position. Raw stat values appear separately in the small stat strip; the bar fills are the position-relative read.

Methodology toggle

Methodology Show methodology
Salary gap

Compare each target salary to the median salary of selected position/role comps. Median is used to reduce distortion from one extreme contract.

2026 normalization

Because 2026 is a CBA/expansion/free-agency reset, salary is also shown as share of the $7.0M cap.

Performance signal

Role, usage, efficiency, position percentiles, on/off impact, and YoY movement are used to classify whether the original value thesis held up.

Accuracy rubric

The story avoids fake precision. A player can be underpaid, market-corrected, mixed, or incomplete. The goal is not to force every pick into a win column — it is to show analyst judgment.

Two tests, one story

Cleaner validation: 2024 stats → 2025 salary. This is the cleanest test of the original undervalued call.

Market reset check: 2025 stats → 2026 salary. Useful, but interpreted as contextual because 2026 salary conditions changed dramatically.

Visual rule: percentile-based skill reads use a gold scale; market/value signals use blue, violet, sand, and coral. That keeps skill strength separate from salary interpretation.

What the self-audit shows

Fiebich and Engstler are the strongest hits. Hillmon looks market-corrected but still below comparable salary. Sabally is the honest mixed case, which actually strengthens the project because it shows the model is being critiqued, not protected.

Best portfolio line: the point is not “my model was always right.” The point is “I know how to revisit a call when the market changes.”

Conclusion

The retrospective supports the original undervalued thesis most clearly for Fiebich and Engstler, reframes Hillmon as a market-corrected hit, and flags Sabally as a partial miss needing the early 2026 check-in. That turns the project from a static class assignment into a real analytics workflow: make the call, define the test, revisit the evidence, and update the conclusion.
